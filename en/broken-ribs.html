Broken or bruised ribs

A fall, a blow to the chest or very severe coughing can break or bruise your ribs. Breathing in and coughing can be very painful, but ribs usually heal on their own. The most important thing during this time is to keep breathing deeply and moving despite the pain: this helps prevent a chest infection.

Symptoms

  • Severe chest pain, especially when breathing in or coughing.
  • Swelling or tenderness over the ribs.
  • Sometimes bruising on the skin.
  • Feeling or hearing a crack if a rib is broken.

What helps?

  • Take regular painkillers after checking with your doctor; breathing and moving are easier when the pain is under control.
  • In the first few days, regularly hold an ice pack or a bag of frozen peas wrapped in a towel to your ribs.
  • Breathe normally and cough when you need to; hold a pillow against your chest.
  • Sleep more upright for the first few nights.

Active cycle of breathing

Do this cycle, recommended in an NHS leaflet, at least 4 times a day, or more often if you are struggling to clear phlegm. It is best done sitting; you can hold a folded towel or pillow to your chest.

  1. Relaxed breathing: Rest one hand on your tummy and breathe slowly in and out through your nose until you feel calm.
  2. Deep breathing: Breathe in deeply enough to feel your ribs expand sideways, hold for 2–3 seconds, then breathe out slowly. Repeat 4 times.
  3. Relaxed breathing again.
  4. Huffing: With your mouth open, breathe out long and steadily, as if steaming up a mirror. When phlegm comes up, take a deep breath in and give a short, sharp breath out.
  5. Relaxed breathing again.
  6. Supported cough: If you have phlegm, cough while pressing a pillow against the broken area.

Exercises at home

Four exercises to keep your lungs and body moving

Lying down or staying still for long periods makes recovery harder. Do not strain yourself, do not lift heavy objects and avoid movements that make your pain worse.

Slow, deep breathing

Sit upright with your hands on your lower ribs. Breathe in through your nose deeply enough to feel your ribs expand sideways, hold for 2–3 seconds and breathe out slowly. Time it for when your painkillers are working.

4 repetitions, at least 4 times a day

Coughing with a pillow for support

When you need to cough, press a pillow or folded towel over the broken area and cough. Do not hold back phlegm: coughing helps prevent a chest infection.

Whenever you need to cough

Moving your shoulders

Now and then during the day, slowly lift your shoulders up and down and roll them backwards. This stops your shoulders and back stiffening; do not push into pain.

Now and then during the day, a few repetitions

Short, frequent walks

Do not lie down or stay still for long periods. Walk little and often, indoors or outside, and build up the time gradually without pushing yourself.

Often through the day

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an X-ray?

Often not. Ribs cannot be put in a cast or splinted like other bones; broken or bruised, they usually heal on their own. Your doctor may arrange imaging if they suspect your lung or another organ has been affected.

Why do breathing exercises matter so much?

Pain can stop you breathing deeply and coughing. If your lungs do not fill properly and phlegm builds up, the risk of a chest infection rises. Regular deep breathing, coughing when needed and staying active help prevent this. The Eastern Association for the Surgery of Trauma (EAST) guideline on multiple rib fractures in older adults also recommends using a breathing exercise device (incentive spirometer), which is well tolerated and cheap, to reduce lung complications; the foundation of pain control is using several pain relief methods together.

When can I go back to work and sport?

Take time off work and rest if you need to. According to an NHS leaflet, avoid heavy lifting and manual work for at least 4 weeks and contact sports for at least 6 weeks. Do not do sport or exercise that makes the pain worse.

How long does healing take?

Broken or bruised ribs usually heal in 2–6 weeks; for fractures it is most often 4–6 weeks. A mild ache may remain for a while afterwards. If your pain has not eased within a few weeks, see your doctor.

Who needs to take extra care?

According to systematic reviews of chest wall injuries, being 65 or older, breaking three or more ribs and having an existing condition, especially heart or lung disease, roughly double the risk of serious outcomes; pneumonia developing later also raises the risk markedly. If you are in one of these groups, you can agree a follow-up plan with your doctor, and seek help without delay if you develop a fever, coloured phlegm or increasing breathlessness.

I smoke. Does it matter?

Yes. Smoking raises the risk of a chest infection; do not smoke while you recover. You can ask your doctor for support to stop.

For pain in the upper back, see the pain between the shoulder blades guide; to strengthen your bones, see the osteoporosis guide.

When should you go to A&E or see a doctor?

  • If the injury happened in a serious accident, such as a car crash
  • If your breathlessness or chest pain is getting worse
  • If you have pain in your tummy or shoulder
  • If you are coughing up blood
  • If you are coughing up yellow or green phlegm, have a high temperature, or feel cold and shivery
  • If your pain has not eased within a few weeks

In the first four situations, call 112 straight away or go to A&E; a broken rib may have damaged your lung, liver or spleen. Do not drive yourself. In the last two, see your doctor soon.
