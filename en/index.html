Longevity analysis

No needles, no lab tests, no genetic tests.The way you move says a lot about how long, and how well, you will live.

I measure these markers in your home, work out your functional age and turn the results into an exercise prescription made for you.

  • 16%Grip strength

    Higher risk of death for every 5 kg drop, a stronger marker than blood pressure.

    Lancet 2015 · 139,691 people
  • 12%Walking speed

    Lower risk of death for every 0.1 m/s faster walking speed.

    JAMA 2011 · 34,485 people
  • 84%One-leg balance

    Higher risk of death in people who can’t stand on one leg for 10 seconds.

    BJSM 2022 · 1,702 people
  • 6 timesRising from the floor

    Higher risk of cardiovascular death with a low score on the sitting-rising test.

    Eur J Prev Cardiol 2026 · 4,282 people

Measured, not guessed. Every indicator is backed by a published study, and you keep your results as a written report.

The good news: you can’t change your genes, but you can improve your strength, balance and walking at any age.

Quick test

What’s your real age?

Four simple tests you can do at home in 2–3 minutes: muscle strength, balance, daily activity and fitness.

Getting startedYour age
18years
1890
  • Do the tests next to a sturdy chair and a wall or table you can hold on to.
  • Stop straight away if you feel dizzy, have chest pain or become short of breath.
  • If you have heart disease, skip the stair test.
1 / 4 · Muscle strengthStand up from a chair 5 times

Sit on a sturdy chair with its back against a wall, arms crossed over your chest. Without using your arms, stand up fully and sit back down 5 times, as fast as you can. You can use the stopwatch below.

10.0seconds
4 s30 s
2 / 4 · BalanceStand on one leg for 10 seconds

Stand next to a wall with your arms by your sides, looking straight ahead. Rest the toes of the lifted foot against the back of your other calf. You have 3 attempts on each foot.

3 / 4 · Daily activityHow many steps do you take a day on average?

Check last week’s average in your phone’s health app. If you don’t know, estimate: a brisk 30-minute walk is about 3,000–4,000 steps.

5,000steps / day
015,000+
4 / 4 · FitnessHow long does it take you to climb four flights of stairs?

About 60 steps, without stopping: quickly, but without running. If you haven’t tried recently, choose your best estimate.

If you have heart disease, chest pain or shortness of breath, don’t do this test; choose the “I haven’t tried” option.

    The meaning of the logo

    Brain, spine and a three-armed Y

    My registered logo represents my professional journey and my holistic view of recovery.

    Brain

    The brain, which forms the main shape of the logo, expresses the importance of neuroscience, mental processes and mental training in recovery. The brain is not just a centre that directs the body; it also plays an important part in how pain is perceived, in relearning movement, in motivation and in adapting to the recovery process.

    That is why I see mental training and neuroplasticity, the brain’s capacity to change, as an inseparable part of physical recovery.

    Spine

    The spine at the centre of the brain symbolises the body’s main load-bearing axis, posture, movement, balance and the neural communication between brain and body. It also draws inspiration from manual therapy approaches built around the spine, such as osteopathy and chiropractic.

    The three arms of the Y

    Besides the two universities where I studied, the three arms of the “Y” in the logo represent three valued scientists who mentored me in my professional development, trained me, and in whose clinical studies I worked as an assistant:

    • Prof. Dr. Nezih Hekim
    • Prof. Dr. Kemal Nuri Özerkan
    • Prof. Dr. Cihan Aksoy

    Two Ys and the colour blend

    The two “Y” shapes and the way their colours blend into each other express the philosophy of Yin–Yang: the complementary sides of Eastern and Western medicine.

    The yellow tone: Healing is voltage

    The yellow tone of the Y, reminiscent of electricity, refers to the body’s electrical and biological communication processes. It brings electrotherapy from physiotherapy together with acupuncture and dry needling from medicine around one scientific question: how do these methods act through the body’s electricity? It stands for my research into that question, and for a new scientific perspective that might one day reach as far as a Nobel Prize.

    12 vertebrae and Lambda λ

    The 12 vertebrae in the logo symbolise Lambda (λ), Book 12 of Aristotle’s Metaphysics. Turned upside down, the Lambda sign comes close to the “Y” shape and joins the logo’s other meanings.

    1 / 6

    In short, my logo represents the unity of brain and body, the central role of the spine, the union of mental, physical and spiritual healing, the synthesis of East and West, and the three valued mentors who have guided my scientific journey.
