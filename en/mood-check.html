Look after yourself

Check your mood

Look back over the last two weeks with two short, widely used questionnaires: the World Health Organization’s well-being index (WHO-5) and the PHQ-4, which screens for symptoms of anxiety and depression. Nine questions in all, about two minutes. The results do not diagnose anything; they help you see when it may be time to seek support.

The WHO-5 questions and answer options are taken from the official English version of the questionnaire. The developers of the PHQ-4 do not require permission to reproduce or translate it; the questions are shown in their original English wording.

What is the WHO-5?

A five-question measure of well-being over the last two weeks, developed at the World Health Organization’s collaborating centre in mental health. It has been translated into more than 30 languages; according to a review of 213 studies, it is suitable both for depression screening and for monitoring treatment.

The Turkish version was tested with 1,752 adults and older adults in primary care and showed high reliability.

What is the PHQ-4?

A very short screening questionnaire with two questions on anxiety and two on depression. It was developed with 2,149 patients in 15 primary care clinics in the United States. Higher scores went hand in hand with poorer daily functioning, more disability days and more use of health care.

What do the scores mean?

  • WHO-5: 51–100Usual range. The population average is about 70.
  • WHO-5: 50 or belowLow well-being; an assessment for depression is recommended.
  • WHO-5: 28 or belowMatches the level of well-being in patients with major depression.
  • WHO-5: a 10-point changeThe difference between two measurements regarded as meaningful.
  • PHQ-4: 0–2Normal.
  • PHQ-4: 3–5Mild symptoms.
  • PHQ-4: 6–8Moderate symptoms.
  • PHQ-4: 9–12Severe symptoms.
  • Subscores: 3 or moreOn the first two questions for anxiety, or the last two for depression, a more detailed assessment is recommended.

What the result does and does not tell you

These questionnaires do not diagnose; they show whether a more detailed assessment may be needed. A diagnosis is made in a consultation with a doctor or a mental health professional.

  • The result reflects only the last two weeks.
  • Grief, severe stress, lack of sleep or a physical illness can affect the scores.
  • A high symptom score does not mean there is definitely a problem; nor does a low score guarantee that all is well.
  • If you feel unwell, talk to a professional whatever your score.

Frequently asked questions

Where do my answers go?

Nowhere. The scores are calculated on your phone or computer and are not sent to a server. If you choose to save a result, it is kept only in this browser, and you can delete it at any time.

My score was low; do I have depression?

These questionnaires do not diagnose; they are screening tools that show whether a more detailed assessment is needed. A diagnosis is made in a consultation with a doctor or a mental health professional. If your score is below the cut-off, or you don’t feel well, talk to a professional.

How often should I repeat it?

Because the questions cover the last two weeks, repeating it every two weeks is enough. In research, a 10-point change in the well-being score is regarded as meaningful.

My score is fine but I feel bad; what should I do?

Short questionnaires cannot pick up everything. If you feel unwell, or your symptoms are affecting your daily life, see a doctor or a mental health professional whatever your score.

When should you seek help?

  • If you have thoughts of harming yourself or ending your life, call 112 or go to the nearest emergency department straight away.
  • If you have had weeks of unhappiness, hopelessness, loss of interest in things you used to enjoy, or changes in sleep or appetite, see a doctor or a mental health professional.
  • If anxiety or low mood is getting in the way of your work, relationships or daily tasks, don’t put off getting support.
  • If you are worried about someone close to you, talk to them and help them get support.
