How many hours a day do you sit? A large study of data from more than a million people showed that the harmful effect of long hours of sitting varies greatly with how active you are: in people who did 60–75 minutes of moderate activity a day, even sitting for more than 8 hours a day was not linked to a clear increase in the risk of death. How can I sit less?

  • Break up long periods of sitting often: get up and walk for a few minutes every half hour.
  • Stand up or walk around while you are on the phone.
  • Walk or cycle for short journeys.
  • Get up during the ad breaks when watching TV.

Assistive products

  • Desk break timer: reminds you to take breaks from sitting.
  • Brisk walking metronome: brings your walking up to a moderate pace.
  • How much activity is enough?: compare your weekly activity with the recommendations.

Frequently asked questions

How reliable are these results?

The calculator is based on a meta-analysis published in The Lancet that reanalysed data from more than a million adults in 16 studies in the same way. But these are observational studies: they do not prove that sitting or inactivity causes death. Sitting and activity times were mostly self-reported; most participants were over 45 and lived in Western Europe, the US or Australia. Read the results as a general guide, not as a personal risk calculation.

What counts as moderate activity?

In the study, moderate intensity meant activities such as brisk walking at about 5.6 km/h or cycling at 16 km/h. Walking at about 100 steps a minute also counts as moderate intensity.

Why is TV asked about separately?

The same study looked at TV viewing time separately. Watching 3 hours or more a day was linked to a higher risk except in the most active group, while watching 5 hours or more was linked to an increased risk even in the most active group.

How can I reach 60 minutes a day?

It doesn’t have to be done in one go. For example, 20 minutes of brisk walking on the way to work, 15 minutes at lunchtime, and 25 minutes of walking or cycling in the evening. Breaking up long periods of sitting often is also recommended; the desk break timer and brisk walking metronome can help.
