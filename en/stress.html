Look after yourself

What can you do in stressful moments?

Five skills that take a few minutes a day, adapted from the illustrated guide the World Health Organization wrote for everyone, plus a breathing exercise.

Last updated: 28 September 2026

Try it now

Breathing exercise

Slowing your breathing helps your body calm down. Sit somewhere comfortable and let your shoulders drop. Breathe in through your nose as the circle grows and breathe out slowly as it shrinks. When the edge of the circle turns gold, hold your breath.

Breathe in for 4 seconds and out for 6. No holding. Keep going for as long as you like.

One round takes 10 seconds. 6 rounds take about 1 minute.

Do the holds without straining. If you feel dizzy or short of breath, switch to the Calm pace or return to your normal breathing. If you have serious heart or lung disease, ask your doctor before using techniques with breath holds.

Five skills

Small tools for hard moments

These skills are not meant to make stress disappear completely. The aim is to stay grounded even when difficult feelings are there, and to be able to turn back to what matters to you.

  1. 1

    Ground yourself

    When emotions come like a storm, it is like dropping anchor until the storm passes.

    • NoticeQuietly notice what you are thinking and feeling right now.
    • Your bodyPress your feet into the floor, straighten your back, breathe out slowly and stretch your shoulders.
    • Look aroundNotice, one by one, the things you can see, hear and touch around you. Then go back to what you were doing.
  2. 2

    Unhook from difficult thoughts

    Difficult thoughts can catch us like a fishing hook and pull us away from the life we are living right now.

    • NoticeNotice that a thought has hooked you.
    • Name itSay to yourself: “I’m noticing a difficult thought.”
    • RefocusBring your attention back to what you are doing and to the people around you.
  3. 3

    Act on your values

    We cannot always change our circumstances, but we can choose the kind of person we want to be. What matters to you: being patient, kind, helpful?

    • This weekChoose a small, doable step that shows this value, such as calling a friend or helping a neighbour.
  4. 4

    Be kind to yourself

    Treat yourself the way you would treat a friend who is going through a hard time.

    • TouchGently place a hand on your chest and feel its warmth.
    • Say“This is really hard. I am not alone in feeling this way.”
  5. 5

    Make room for feelings

    Fighting a difficult feeling often makes it bigger. Try carrying it with you for a while instead.

    • Be curiousWhere in your body do you feel the emotion? If it were an object, what would its shape, colour and temperature be?
    • Breathe outBreathe out slowly around that area and make room for it. Feelings come and go.

Frequently asked questions

How do you do a breathing exercise when you are stressed?

Sit somewhere comfortable and let your shoulders drop. Breathe in through your nose for 4 seconds and out slowly over 6 seconds. One round takes 10 seconds; 6 rounds take about 1 minute. Slowing your breathing helps your body calm down.

How do you do box breathing and 4-7-8 breathing?

For box breathing, breathe in for 4 seconds, hold for 4, breathe out for 4 and hold for 4. For the 4-7-8 technique, breathe in through your nose for 4 seconds, hold for 7, then breathe out through slightly parted lips over 8 seconds. Do the holds without straining; if you have serious heart or lung disease, ask your doctor before using techniques with breath holds.

What are the five skills in the World Health Organization’s stress guide?

Grounding yourself, unhooking from difficult thoughts, acting on your values, being kind to yourself and making room for feelings. The aim is not to make stress disappear completely, but to be able to turn back to what matters to you even when difficult feelings are there.
